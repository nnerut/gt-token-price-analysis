## What GateToken is, in one paragraph

GT is the native token of Gate, launched in 2019. It runs on GateChain (the exchange's own Layer 1) and also exists as an ERC-20 token. In practice it does four jobs: it cuts trading fees when you choose to pay fees in GT, it counts toward VIP tier eligibility, it gets you into Launchpad and Startup events, and it's the gas token for Gate's Layer 2 network. That last one matters more than it used to — a token used for staking and discounts is a loyalty perk, a token used for gas is a running cost.

## Why the price moved this year

Three things showed up in the reporting around the September rally.

**Platform inflows.** DefiLlama data put Gate's seven-day net inflow at roughly $118.75 million, third among centralized exchanges. That's money arriving at the exchange — a related signal, not proof that it's buying GT.

**Supply shrinking on a schedule.** Gate burns GT quarterly using platform revenue. Reported figures: about 2.57 million GT burned in Q2 2026, with cumulative burns reaching roughly 190 million GT — around 63% of the original 300 million supply. Burning doesn't create demand, but it does mean less supply has to absorb whatever demand exists.

**Broad crypto recovery.** Exchange tokens tend to move as a levered version of market sentiment. When bitcoin recovered, GT followed.

## The caveat that belongs in any honest price discussion

GT's 24-hour spot volume readings across trackers were in the $2M–$5.1M range on several September pages. For a token with a market cap over $1.2 billion, that's a thin book.

Thin means two-way. Rising 8% in a day is easy; giving it back is also easy. One Gate Square write-up from early September described the print as having only around $3 million of daily turnover and flagged that as a double-edged setup — momentum that looks convincing on a chart and wobbles on a moderately sized sell order. Worth remembering when you see a chart that only goes back 90 days.

## Supply and all-time records, where sources disagree

The supply numbers are a mess, and it's better to say so than to pick one and pretend:

- **Circulating supply:** readings of 106.6 million, 110 million, 112.6 million, 115.2 million and 118.87 million GT appear across Binance, CoinGecko, Yahoo Finance, The Block and Gate's own price page.
- **Max supply:** Binance and Crypto.com list about 142.6 million GT. Gate's own page showed a total supply near 119 million. The original cap was 300 million, reduced by burns.
- **All-time high:** about $25.38 (CoinGecko, Mixin, The Block) or $25.95 (Binance, Yahoo) — both pinned to January 25, 2025.
- **All-time low:** roughly $0.26 in March 2020.

Data providers compute "supply" differently (locked tokens, treasury, bridged versions), which is why they never agree. Treat any single number as approximate.

## The risks, stated plainly

GT is an exchange token. Its value is tied to one company's trading volume, product launches and regulatory standing. If Gate's business slows, GT doesn't have a second engine. The drawdown history is real: from the January 2025 high near $25, GT traded as low as the mid-$6s in 2026 — roughly a 73% drop at the low. Quarterly burns reduce supply but prove nothing about future demand. And a market cap above $1.2 billion with single-digit-millions daily volume means slippage and volatility on both sides.
